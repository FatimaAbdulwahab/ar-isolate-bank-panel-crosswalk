## What you are building, in one sentence

A free, citable table that shows, for every reference organism CDC and FDA sell in their Antimicrobial Resistance Isolate Bank, which resistance genes it carries and which rapid blood culture panel targets it can be used to check.

## Why it matters

Before a laboratory reports a new test on patients, federal rules (42 CFR 493.1253) say it must first show the test works in its own hands. Most laboratories do that with well-characterized reference organisms, and the Isolate Bank is the main free U.S. source of them. But the Bank is organized by panel, not by test. A laboratory bringing up a rapid blood culture panel has to open isolate pages one at a time to learn which organisms carry KPC, which carry OXA-48-like, and which are the tricky ones where the gene and the growth result disagree. Think of it as a library with no catalog: everything is on the shelves, but you have to pull every book to find what you need. This project is the catalog.

## Where the information comes from

1. **The CDC and FDA Antimicrobial Resistance Isolate Bank website** (https://wwwn.cdc.gov/ARIsolateBank/). One row there is one isolate: its Bank number, species, NCBI BioSample number, the genes CDC found, and CDC's reference broth microdilution results. Free and public.
2. **FDA decision summaries** for each of the seven cleared rapid blood culture panels with resistance targets (BioFire BCID2, ePlex BCID-GP and BCID-GN, Verigene BC-GP and BC-GN, LIAISON PLEX Gram-Positive and Gram-Negative). These list exactly which resistance targets each panel reports. Free and public.
3. **NCBI Pathogen Detection**, the federal genome database. It runs NCBI's own gene finder (AMRFinderPlus) on the same genomes, the same tool your Project 1 benchmark used. Free and public.
4. **Your Project 1 files**: the breakpoint table you already confirmed and the gene-to-panel-target crosswalk.

## What you will have at the end

- A public project page on GitHub with the full crosswalk.
- A spreadsheet-ready table: every isolate, its genes, its panel targets, its reference results, and a flag for the hard cases.
- A panel-by-target grid: which Bank panels exercise which rapid panel targets.
- A permanent citation number (DOI) from Zenodo.
- The results of an outside check of your Project 1 benchmark against CDC's reference results, ready to become the preprint.

## How it connects to your work

You ran verification, quality control and proficiency testing under CLIA, CAP and Joint Commission rules, and your rapid diagnostics review names the Isolate Bank as a verification resource. This turns that recommendation into a tool other laboratories can use, and it gives your Project 1 benchmark an independent test.
