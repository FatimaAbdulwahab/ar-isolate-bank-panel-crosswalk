# Limitations

*Plain-language note: the honest list of what this project cannot tell you. Any paper or post about it must stay inside these limits.*

1. **The crosswalk says which targets an isolate carries, not that a given panel will detect it.** Allele-level detection (for example a rare OXA-48 variant, or IMP alleles a primer set may miss) is defined by each manufacturer's instructions for use. The crosswalk tells a laboratory which organisms are candidates; the instructions for use decide whether each is a valid positive.
2. **CDC's gene lists come from several methods and dates.** The note on each isolate's gene tab says how genes were called: ResFinder with its June 2016 database (289 isolates), ARG-ANNOT plus ResFinder in 2018 (40), ResFinder 3.0 (30), a combined ResFinder, ARG-ANNOT and NCBI database accessed in 2021 or 2022 (362, some naming NCBI AMRFinder as the primary database), or no method stated (141). None include point mutations. The method for each isolate is in the `cdc_gene_method` column. Older calls can miss genes added to databases later. CDC also sometimes appends a bracket note to a gene name (for example "OXA-48(c)"); the crosswalk ignores the note and records it in `reference/cdc_gene_to_target.csv`.
3. **The gene-call comparison is only partly independent.** Check A compares CDC's lists with NCBI's AMRFinderPlus. For isolates CDC called with a database that includes NCBI's, the two are not independent. Agreement is reported separately for isolates CDC called without NCBI's database (`external_check_A_by_cdc_method.csv`), and it is just as high there. The few disagreements (four OXA-232 and two CTX-M-15 listed by CDC but not found by NCBI) are most likely genes on small plasmids that are missing from the public assembly.
4. **MREJ cannot be read from a gene list.** The BCID2 "MRSA" result needs both mecA/C and the SCCmec junction (MREJ). The crosswalk assumes every mecA- or mecC-positive S. aureus is an expected MRSA positive. MREJ variants that the assay may not detect are not assessed, so a laboratory should not treat these isolates as confirmed MRSA-call positives without the manufacturer's information.
5. **Verigene BC-GN OXA coverage is inferred.** FDA's decision summary lists "OXA" without naming groups. The crosswalk takes OXA-23 and OXA-48 groups from the strains used in its analytical studies. The package insert, which was not available here, is the authority.
6. **Organism identification is CDC's.** Species names are taken as posted. A few names on CDC pages are older (for example Enterobacter aerogenes) and are grouped by Project 1's rules.
7. **The Bank is not a patient population.** It is built to contain unusual, difficult and multi-mechanism organisms. Prevalence-dependent numbers (PPV, NPV, and sensitivity of a single gene) in the outside check describe this collection, not what a laboratory will see in its patients.
8. **Small numbers for some targets.** vanA, vanB, VIM, IMP and mecC have 2 to 36 isolates; their ranges are wide.
9. **CDC's posted interpretation is used for hard cases.** Each CDC page names the edition it used: CLSI M100 2025 for most isolates, but 2020, 2024 and 2026 editions for others (counts in `paper/stats.json`, `int_standards`), and FDA breakpoints where a footnote says so. A laboratory using different breakpoints may classify some isolates differently; the crosswalk keeps every MIC so users can reinterpret. The outside check reinterprets with Project 1's single breakpoint table.
10. **The consistency check is not inter-laboratory.** The 24 Bank isolates also in the Project 1 benchmark were all deposited in NCBI by CDC. Comparing their NCBI antibiograms with CDC's web pages checks CDC's two postings against each other; it says nothing about agreement between laboratories.
11. **Not every isolate is in NCBI Pathogen Detection.** 31 Bank isolates in Project 1's organisms had no AMRFinderPlus result, and 112 Bank isolates have no BioSample listed. They stay in the crosswalk but not in the outside check.
12. **Website snapshot.** The Bank adds isolates and revises MICs (each page has a change history). This release reflects pages downloaded on 2026-10-24; the provenance log fingerprints each page.
13. **Scope.** Fungal, gonococcal, C. difficile and enteric-pathogen panels are listed but not mapped, because cleared rapid blood culture panels do not report resistance genes for those organisms.
14. **No physical isolates were tested.** Everything here comes from published CDC, FDA and NCBI data.
